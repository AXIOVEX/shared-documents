# Employer skill gaps and bridges that preserve employment

## Begin with a receiving role, not a course catalog

The pathways below are proposals. No employer has confirmed a requisition, placement, pay level or training duration for this report. A skills similarity screen must be followed by an individual assessment and a real job requirement. Employers may need better job design, wages or mentoring rather than a new external credential.

| Existing experience | Candidate adjacent work | Transferable strengths | Bridge and practical verification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Production operator | Quality / automation support | Process knowledge, abnormal-condition recognition | Measurement, sensors, safety and fault isolation; inspect a real part/process under supervision |
| Maintenance technician | Reliability / controls support | Troubleshooting, mechanical/electrical familiarity | Condition monitoring, PLC diagnosis and industrial networks; diagnose a supervised fault |
| Machinist / toolmaker | CAD/CAM / advanced metrology | Materials, tooling, tolerances | Digital process planning, inspection programming; produce and inspect a specified part |
| Data-entry / administrative worker | Data stewardship / operations specialist | Process knowledge, records and customer context | Spreadsheets/SQL, validation, exceptions; find seeded errors and repair the workflow |
| Bookkeeping / finance clerk | Financial operations / controls support | Reconciliation and transaction knowledge | ERP, analytics, control evidence; resolve exceptions with an audit trail |
| Customer-service worker | Complex support / service quality | Communication, product knowledge and escalation | Case analysis, tool evaluation, knowledge maintenance; resolve a nonstandard case |
| Software developer / junior programmer | Secure AI-assisted engineering | Code, debugging and system knowledge | Requirements, tests, threat modeling and evaluation; defend a tested change against adversarial cases |
| Mechanical drafter | Product definition / manufacturing quality | Drawings, geometry and design intent | Tolerances, manufacturing methods, metrology; verify drawing-to-part conformity |

Sources for occupational context: S09, S12, S13; for skill taxonomy: S17; for imaging education: S15. The bridges are analyst-designed hypotheses, not validated transition probabilities or substitutes for licensure. A programmer-to-engineer bridge may require substantial foundational development; an office role does not become a regulated profession through a short course.

## A job-continuity operating sequence

1. Obtain a voluntary aggregate signal of tasks at risk and tasks needing capacity. Keep personal worker records out of public reporting.
2. Assess demonstrated skills against a receiving employer's job requirements, including shift, location, pay, hours, benefits and role-specific credentials.
3. Prefer internal redeployment, then voluntary external placement. Agree a learning plan, mentor, assessment and realistic start date before treating the pathway as available.
4. Use paid supervised work where feasible. Determine duration from the assessed gap; do not promise a universal six-week transition.
5. Measure competence and early performance, then sustained employment and compensation. Adjust training if its outcomes miss the agreed goals.

For aerospace and defense, role-specific quality, traceability, controlled-data and security requirements need employer confirmation. A general AI course is not a clearance or compliance certification. No funding eligibility or legal entitlement is asserted here.

## A practical employer-university feedback loop

Create a monthly voluntary sector review with employers, workers, educators and workforce organizations. Use aggregate requisitions and validated competency rubrics; avoid coordination of wages or restrictions on worker movement. Universities can update modules from repeated skill evidence while employers supply supervised projects and feedback.

Pool demand only after deduplicating openings by employer, role, location and intended start window. Distinguish a vacancy, an expression of interest and a funded hiring commitment. Track actual work requirements rather than inflating every posting with desirable credentials. Give workers a way to challenge an inaccurate assessment.

## Proposed 90-day implementation

| Window | Action | Exit evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1-30 | Review several employers' real roles, program maps and worker constraints | De-identified requirements, assessed gaps, viable receiving roles and consent |
| Days 31-60 | Run small paid bridge cohorts and revise curriculum modules | Attendance, practical assessments, mentor feedback, recorded cost and compensation |
| Days 61-90 | Validate starts/retention and decide whether to expand | Confirmed work, wage/hours/benefits comparison, unresolved gaps and public aggregate results |

No fixed cohort size or dollar budget is claimed as evidence. A proposed budget should separately price coordination, instruction, paid learning time, supports and evaluation. Allocate funding sources to distinct allowable costs and verify current program terms with administrators before commitments. Historical funding descriptions in the prior edition are not current award availability.

## Measure outcomes that matter

Report 6- and 12-month sustained employment, occupational match, involuntary part-time status, wage/hours/benefits change, completion, employer-assessed competence and Michigan residence/work location where consent and lawful access permit. Denominators must show everyone offered support, enrolled, completed and followed up, with attrition and missing outcomes visible.

Cost per sustained placement equals incremental program cost divided by verified sustained placements under a published definition. Jobs saved requires a credible comparison of what would have happened without the intervention; participants retained is not automatically jobs saved. Use a pre-specified comparison design where feasible and show baseline differences and uncertainty. Track worker satisfaction and equitable access as well as employer productivity.

The industrial benefit to test is better use of existing skills, faster verified placement, lower avoidable turnover and a stronger education-to-work pipeline. This report proposes how to measure those benefits; it does not claim they have already occurred.
