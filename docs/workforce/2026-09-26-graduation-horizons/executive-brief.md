# Michigan workforce: prepare for changed work before graduation

**Decision brief | Evidence cutoff September 26, 2026, America/Detroit**

Michigan should connect curriculum redesign with paid worker transitions and verified employer demand. Neither the unemployment rate nor an AI capability demonstration is sufficient to decide which programs to expand or close.

## What the new evidence says

Michigan's August seasonally adjusted unemployment rate was **5.0%**, against **4.1% nationally**. Michigan participation was **58.8%** and its employment-population ratio **55.9%**. The state's monthly employment and labor-force declines both totaled **25,000**. A rate moving only slightly can therefore conceal a material change in participation. [S01, S03]

Michigan's broader U-6 measure was **9.1%** for the July 2025-June 2026 window excluding October 2025. This is not an August reading. Nationally, recent graduates' second-quarter unemployment was about **5.6%**, and degree-based underemployment was **42%**. These measure different populations and cannot be added. [S04, S05]

September's final national consumer sentiment index fell to **48.1**, from **51.7** in August. Chicago Fed district respondents reported below-average hiring indexes even as manufacturing activity strengthened. These are warning signals to examine alongside payrolls, hires, hours and employer orders, not a forecast of Michigan job losses. [S06, S07]

## Five decisions for education and industry

1. **Redesign narrow task training first.** Standalone typing, data-entry and routine transaction-processing curricula need immediate review. Preserve transferable business knowledge while adding data quality, controls, exception handling and customer problem-solving. Review mechanical drafting and legacy production specializations with local employers before reducing capacity.
2. **Retain foundations in AI-exposed professions.** Software, accounting, legal support, engineering, media and medical imaging face task change. Teach students to define requirements, evaluate outputs, manage risk and work with accountable professionals. AI exposure alone does not establish occupation decline. [S10]
3. **Prioritize Michigan demand and replacement needs.** State projections show growth in industrial maintenance, cybersecurity, software, data science, nursing and electrical work. Declining occupations can still have substantial annual openings. Match program capacity to verified regional demand and placement outcomes; national projections alone are insufficient. [S09, S12, S13]
4. **Build skills bridges before layoffs.** Assess incumbent skills, obtain a real receiving role, pay for supervised learning, and verify competence through work samples. Track wages, benefits, hours and sustained employment, not just course completion.
5. **Plan by graduation cohort.** Review short credentials for 2027, associate pathways for 2028, bachelor's programs for 2030, and longer professional pathways for 2032 and beyond. These are decision horizons, not occupation expiry dates.

## Radiology illustrates adaptation, not a universal prediction

The official projections do not establish radiologist obsolescence. Michigan projects growth for physician radiologists and radiologic technologists; ACR describes persistent workforce shortages. Preserve clinical preparation and add role-specific AI evaluation, imaging informatics, monitoring and workflow assurance. AI proficiency does not confer new clinical privileges. [S09, S12, S14, S15]

## Implementation and limits

In the next 90 days, convene employer/program reviews, validate several paid transition cohorts, and establish a common outcome dashboard. No named institution or employer is committed by this report. The companion provides program decisions, cross-sector AI analysis, worker pathways and evaluation rules. No program is declared obsolete on a speculative date.

This edition uses newly reviewed public research with its own evidence ledger. It does not claim a refresh of the unavailable operational database. County API observations were available through July, whereas state observations reached August. Historical university outcomes and price comparisons were not reharvested. Sources, data vintages, corroboration and unresolved gaps are documented in the evidence appendix.
