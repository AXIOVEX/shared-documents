# University programs for the jobs available after graduation

## Decision method

Programs should build durable expertise and the ability to verify AI-assisted work. Match curriculum decisions to when students complete their training. State projections are useful planning inputs, not promises; annual openings include replacement demand. A growing national occupation can contract locally, and an occupation in net decline can still hire. [S09, S12, S13]

The generated occupation table pairs Michigan growth with annual openings. A separate regional column retains its older vintage. Do not average these projection rates or treat their difference as acceleration. Radiologists' small rounded state counts particularly require caution; no seats-to-openings quota is inferred.

## Graduation horizons

| Entering in 2026 | Planning horizon | Curriculum decision now | Evidence to review before expansion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Short credential / incumbent bridge | 2027 | Teach immediate supervised tasks; attach to verified work | Requisition, mentor, pay/hours, practical assessment |
| Associate / technical pathway | 2028 | Add controls, data literacy, quality and work experience | Regional openings, employer placements, credential requirements |
| Bachelor's degree | 2030 | Preserve fundamentals; integrate domain-specific AI, security and verification | Cohort occupational match, placement capacity, current state projections |
| Graduate / professional pathway | 2032 and beyond | Retain depth, supervised practice, research methods and accountability | Licensure/accreditation, residency/clinical capacity, workforce projections and access needs |

These are approximate completion horizons, not guarantees about individual completion or work availability. Review curricula annually and task-level modules each term when tools change. Do not redesign an entire degree around the current interface of one AI product.

## What should be scrapped, and when?

The strongest immediate candidates for retirement are **obsolete learning outcomes**, such as awarding a credential primarily for manual transcription of structured data or unverified execution of repetitive software commands. Replace these in the next curriculum review cycle, proposed for 2026-27. That is an action date, not a prediction that every related job disappears then.

For complete programs, use **conditional teach-out**, not a list derived only from AI exposure. A proposed review rule is to consider reducing new intake when multiple graduate cohorts show persistently poor occupational placement or earnings, local employer demand including replacement openings is weak, and redesign cannot provide a credible pathway at acceptable cost. Check sample size, survey response bias, public-service needs and student circumstances before interpreting outcomes.

No occupational obsolescence date is established by the reviewed evidence. For data entry, routine clerical work, narrowly defined coding, drafting and some legacy production roles, official declines warrant redesign now and an annual intake review. For accounting, software engineering, legal support and creative programs, broad professional foundations may remain valuable even as entry tasks change. A degree maps to multiple occupations, so do not equate one declining SOC code with a whole academic discipline.

Any closure proposal must include: completion protection for enrolled students; transfer/credit recognition; faculty development or redeployment; total student costs; access obligations; independent employer review; and a published alternative pathway. Do not strand students midway through a credential.

## How to size programs without inventing a skills-gap count

Start with detailed occupation codes and the official CIP-SOC relationship, recognizing its many-to-many structure. Review Michigan and regional openings, then confirm what portion local employers actually expect to fill with new graduates, incumbent promotions, experienced hires or migrants. Credential awards are not unique graduates or available workers. Avoid summing overlapping occupation demand into several programs at once. [S12, S13, S18]

A proposed seat model is: target annual placements divided by expected completion and verified placement rates, then capped by instructional/clinical capacity. All rates must come from observed, appropriately defined cohorts. This edition lacks sufficient local inputs to compute seats or name a particular university program for closure. Publish the input assumptions and a sensitivity range when those inputs exist.

Candidate university and community-college reviews should be open to the institutions already cataloged in the repository. Their existing program listings or historical outcomes do not establish current seats, partnership, endorsement or willingness to participate.
